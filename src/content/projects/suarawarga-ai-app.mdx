## The Problem Context

Traditional emergency reporting relies heavily on manual data entry and phone dispatchers, which introduces critical friction during peak disaster events. When individuals are in distress, typing precise locations or filling out forms is often unfeasible.

The objective of SuaraWarga was to bridge the gap between unstructured human panic (voice) and structured, actionable emergency data (maps).

## System Architecture

To achieve a seamless transition from voice to map, the pipeline was broken down into three distinct modules:

1. Audio Ingestion: Capturing raw audio streams from the user interface.
2. Transcription and Extraction: Utilizing AI models to transcribe the audio and extract key entities (location, disaster type, severity).
3. GIS Integration: Mapping the extracted coordinates to a real-time visualization dashboard.

### Prompt Engineering Constraints

One of the most significant technical hurdles was ensuring the AI model reliably extracted geographic data without hallucinating coordinates. For the MVP, strict prompt constraints were established:

- Zero-shot extraction: The model was instructed to return data strictly in JSON format.
- Geographic bounding: Validation checks ensured extracted locations fell within expected regional coordinates.

Example expected output:

~~~json
  {
    "incident_type": "flood",
    "severity": "high",
    "location_context": "Main intersection near the central market",
    "estimated_coordinates": [-6.9932, 110.4203]
  }
~~~
## Key Results

In controlled prototype tests:
- Median time from report submission to mapped incident preview dropped from around 3 minutes (manual process) to under 40 seconds.
- Parsing success for incident type and severity reached around 92 percent after prompt constraint tuning.
- Invalid coordinate outputs were reduced significantly after bounding and post-validation steps.

## Technical Decisions

- Chose strict JSON output contracts to make parser logic deterministic.
- Added a validation layer after LLM extraction, rather than trusting model output directly.
- Prioritized fast response over perfect transcription quality because emergency triage values speed first.

## Challenges and Tradeoffs

- Noisy audio and mixed local dialects reduced transcription quality in some samples.
- Aggressive prompt constraints improved consistency but sometimes removed useful narrative detail.
- Geographic disambiguation remained difficult when users mentioned landmarks without street-level references.

## Next Iteration

Planned improvements:
- Add confidence scoring per extracted field and route low-confidence reports to manual review.
- Incorporate retrieval-based place resolution for landmarks and local aliases.
- Add multilingual prompt templates and test coverage for region-specific speech patterns.
